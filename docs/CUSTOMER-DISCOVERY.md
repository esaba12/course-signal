# Post-Hackathon Customer Discovery Guide

## Objective

Learn whether this signal would improve a real course-planning workflow before expanding data access or adding product features.

## Who to interview

- Undergraduate advising leaders.
- Department course schedulers / curriculum staff.
- Department chairs or program directors.
- Registrar/scheduling operations staff.

Do not begin by pitching the model. Begin by understanding the existing decision.

## 20-minute interview flow

1. “Walk us through the last time you planned or reviewed course offerings.”
2. “What information did you use? What was missing or hard to trust?”
3. “Which decisions are recurrent, and when are they made?”
4. Show the historical trend and backtest only. “Would this change any part of that conversation? Why or why not?”
5. “What context would make this misleading or unsafe to use?”
6. “If you could add one authorized data source, what would it be and who governs it?”
7. “What would make a pilot useful enough to continue?”

## What to record

- Exact decision/workflow moment.
- Existing data and systems used.
- Trust objections and missing context.
- Required data owner/approval path.
- Measurable value hypothesis, such as time saved or earlier exception detection.

## Avoid

- Asking an interviewee to endorse the product.
- Claiming the forecast is demand, capacity, or a staffing recommendation.
- Requesting student-level data before documenting a specific need and approval path.
